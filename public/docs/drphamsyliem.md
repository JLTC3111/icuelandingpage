Dr. Phạm Sỹ Liêm was a Vietnamese civil engineer, scholar, government leader and influential advocate for urban development reform. His career spanned university teaching, senior public administration, scientific research and the establishment of professional institutions, including ICUE.
Born in 1931 in Diễn Thành, Diễn Châu, Nghệ An, he died on November 30, 2018, aged 87 (88 by traditional Vietnamese age reckoning).

Life and career at a glance
Period	Career and contributions
1950s–1960s	Taught civil engineering and helped establish reinforced-concrete engineering education at Hanoi University of Science and Technology.
Later government career	Served as Deputy Minister of Construction and Vice Chairman of the Hanoi People's Committee.
From 1982	Founding president of the Vietnam Federation of Civil Engineering Associations, leading its first three terms and later serving as vice president.
1990s–2000s	Expanded his work in urban governance, infrastructure and construction policy research.
1999	Founded the Institute for Urban Research and Infrastructure Development.
2006	Co-founded Người Đô Thị, a magazine dedicated to urban affairs, and chaired its professional advisory board.
2013	Founded and became inaugural director of the Institute for Construction and Urban Economics (ICUE).
2017–2018	Continued researching and advising professional organizations despite his advanced age. Published his final major book in September 2018.
His government posts, federation leadership and research work are independently documented in contemporary obituaries and professional memorials.

His most significant documented achievements
1. Building professional engineering institutions. He was a founding leader of the Vietnam Federation of Civil Engineering Associations and helped develop its organizational structure, research activities and professional influence over decades.
2. Establishing two urban research institutes. He founded the Institute for Urban Research and Infrastructure Development and later ICUE, creating organizations dedicated to research and policy advice on urban planning, construction economics and infrastructure.
3. Contributing to Vietnam's urban development discourse. Through books, research projects, conferences and interviews, he regularly examined housing, land management, public infrastructure and urban governance. His work included public criticism of weaknesses in planning and policy implementation.
4. Co-founding Người Đô Thị in 2006. The publication became a platform for discussions of urban policy and quality of life. He remained professionally involved with the magazine until his death.
5. Publishing Tân kinh tế học thể chế (New Institutional Economics) in 2018. His final major academic work explored the relationship between institutions and economic development. Published when he was 87, it reportedly drew on more than 200 foreign references alongside Vietnamese research and policy documents.

Intellectual life and personal legacy
His colleagues remembered a scholar who continued learning long after reaching senior government office. He reportedly spoke four foreign languages: English, French, Russian and Chinese.
At a 2019 memorial event, federation chairman Trần Ngọc Hùng recalled that when travelling abroad, Dr. Liêm would seek out bookshops. He maintained an extensive personal library and read widely, even enjoying martial-arts fiction.
Academician Phạm Hồng Giang characterized his legacy through three qualities: personal integrity, intellectual depth and an exceptional capacity for work. Those are colleagues' assessments, rather than independently measured achievements, but they help explain the professional respect he received.

Two particularly important historical records
One of his earliest documented achievements deserves special recognition: he was appointed the first head of the Reinforced Concrete Department at Hanoi University of Science and Technology in October 1959. The university's construction faculty records confirm that he helped introduce the subject in the 1958–1959 academic year, began postgraduate study in the Soviet Union in 1963, and subsequently continued his academic leadership.

His contributions to public policy are also supported by original evidence rather than memorial tributes alone. In 2012, he submitted detailed criticism of the draft Land Law through VCCI, addressing land acquisition, property rights and transparency. In 2016–2017, he participated in public discussions of the Planning Law, arguing for more coordinated national planning and stronger institutional reform.
